# Merge six-month market-entry simulation

_Generated by `npm run sim -w @merge/api` (seed 42, 26 weeks from 2026-11-02, 2-hour steps). 53,489 API calls in 17.6 minutes of machine time. Re-run after changes to see what moved._

## What was simulated

A staffing agency ("Merge Staffing") launches the platform with a bank of 25 workers, growing to 45. Twenty care providers join over 18 weeks: two large groups (400 and 250 staff, 8 and 5 sites), six medium homes and twelve small services. Employers with 40 or more staff onboard by spreadsheet import (with email invitations and bulk verification of what the sheet did not attest); smaller ones add each worker by hand (one record, six to eight documents, a verification each). Each week every provider's staffing balance drifts with winter pressure (peaking mid-January) and random shocks; short-staffed providers post shifts with realistic lead times (55% three to ten days ahead, 25% one to three days, 20% same day), over-staffed providers raise surplus flags, suppliers and self-service workers check the board at their own rhythm and put eligible workers forward, hosts confirm first-come-first-served, workers clock in and out, hosts approve or dispute timesheets, and the agency issues monthly statements. Scripted events test a worker changing employer, a host blocking an unreliable supplier, the agency bank being released first for four weeks, a CQC-style records request, a non-paying host being suspended, a data-subject request, and a provider leaving.

Every action is a real HTTP call to the API as that person would make it, so "calls per task" below is a direct measure of how laborious the task is in the product today.

## What the numbers say

- **30% of shifts started with nobody confirmed.** 434 of 1445 shifts went unfilled. The network never had enough spare, compliant, in-range staff for winter demand, and 611 shifts were escalated automatically as they neared their start (rate raised within the host's ceiling, reach widened); of the 610 that had started by the end of the run, 396 (65%) started with someone confirmed and 214 still went unfilled. 20% of shifts were posted within 12 hours of starting, which collapses the priority windows and turns the exchange into a scramble.
- **The agency bank filled 33% of bookings; priority access filled 17%.** Surplus flags did earn first access, but flagged organisations were small and their flags lapsed; the bank, which is always flagged in effect and checks the board more often, took the larger share. If the agency's aim is to grow a provider-to-provider exchange rather than its own agency business, the product needs to make flagging effortless and reward it.
- **Releasing the agency bank first did not improve fill rate; it displaced priority organisations and slowed filling.** Four weeks before the experiment: agency 29%, priority 18%, unfilled 31%, median time to fill 720 min. During (agency FIRST): agency 34%, priority 13%, unfilled 30%, time to fill 720 min. After: agency 33%, priority 13%, unfilled 29%, time to fill 720 min. The bank took bookings that flagged providers would otherwise have had, and hosts waited longer because the bank's exclusive hour delayed everyone else.
- **2 organisation(s) ended the run suspended or gone.** Lakeside Lodge, Birch House. Suspension withdrew their open shifts and told the hosts holding their staff (PLT-01); confirmed bookings of their staff elsewhere were honoured pending the owner's decision, and leaving the network is still a request to the agency with no final statement or data export (PLT-02).
- **46,885 notifications generated, 32,816 emails sent (immediate and daily digest).** Peak week 2027-03-08: 3,811 notifications across the network, mostly release notices. Urgent shifts, late cancellations and disputes went out by email at once; the rest were batched into each person's daily digest, so nobody had to be logged in to hear about them.

### Security & data

- **PT-01** · Open sign-up gives an unchecked stranger care-home addresses and staff door codes in three calls — New organisations register as PENDING and must be approved by the platform before they can publish or receive shifts.
- **PT-03** · No Microsoft sign-in, no two-factor, no lockout, weak passwords allowed, passwords that can never be changed, and leavers can still log in — Authenticator-app two-factor is built in and compulsory for admins and managers, accounts lock after repeated failed sign-ins, passwords must be at least 10 characters and not common, and passwords can be changed or reset by email; Microsoft single sign-on is deferred to after go-live (decision 16).
- **PH-02** · Deactivating a worker leaves their login live: a leaver can still see bookings and submit timesheets — A worker login now ends when the worker record is deactivated (requireAuth and login check the linked worker). Cancelling or reassigning a leaver's future bookings remains open; see WF-02.
- **PR-01** · Site access notes (door code, who to report to) are released to the whole network before any booking — Site access notes are released only to an organisation with a confirmed booking on that shift.
- **PT-04** · Any worker login can read a colleague's pay, DBS number and postcode, and add or remove that colleague's unavailability — Worker logins can no longer read or edit colleagues' records; organisation-wide worker access needs a manager role.
- **PT-05** · The audit log cannot show what one user did last month — GET /organisations/:id/audit?userId=&from=&to= filters the audit log by user and period.
- **SEC-01** · Data-subject tooling: export, erasure request, erasure with records kept, consent recorded — Kamil Ahmed read their profile (200), downloaded everything held about them (200: 7 document(s) including superseded versions, 0 assignment(s), timesheets, ratings, bars, reports, login and consent dates) and asked to be forgotten (202); Riverside Living's admin found the request in their inbox. ### Compliance

- **CMP-03** · Workers add their own documents; the employer verifies them from a queue — Oscar Robinson (Harmony Supported Living) added a renewed training certificate from their own login (201, verified: no, it waits in the employer's verification queue); a file can be attached to it, and a correction keeps the old version as history. Files go through a storage adapter (local disk in development, an S3-compatible bucket in a UK region in production) and are kept 6 years after the worker leaves by default; that retention period still needs the lawyer's confirmation (decision 18).
- **PC-03** · Compliance evidence is editable in place with no history visible to the employer, so a point-in-time check cannot be proven — Correcting a document supersedes the old version instead of editing it in place; the history stays on the record (and in the worker's export) and a verified document cannot be deleted.
- **PM-01** · A manager cannot bar one worker from the home, and blocking their whole employer does not cover shifts already posted — A host bars a named worker from all or one of its sites with a reason (POST /trust/bars); future bookings with that host are released and the employer is told.
- **PM-02** · Feedback on borrowed workers goes nowhere: rating comments can never be read back, the supplier is not told, and there is no incident record — A host reports a concern about a lent worker to the employer (POST /trust/reports); the employer acknowledges and closes it, and the report stays on the worker's record (decision 14).
- **PR-02** · Host cannot see any compliance evidence for a borrowed worker, and skill tags such as 'Medication' are unverified — A host sees a "meets mandatory requirements" compliance status for each candidate and booked worker rather than the documents (decision 11), and can state its own requirements (GET/POST /compliance/requirements) against which suppliers declare their workers.
- **CMP-01** · A verification queue shows what is unverified and clears it in bulk — Mosaic Community Support ended onboarding with 1 document(s) never verified; GET /compliance/verification-queue listed 1 and POST /compliance/verify cleared 1 in 1 call(s), so no worker stays blocked without the officer knowing. - **PC-06** · No host-specific clearance: Oakwood cannot require or record its site induction, and 'worked here before' shows 0 for workers who did — Hosts define their own requirements, such as a site induction (POST /compliance/requirements), and record which workers meet them (POST /compliance/requirements/:id/workers).
- **PH-04** · No expiry-horizon report: finding DBS checks expiring in 60 days means opening 413 profiles — GET /compliance/expiring?days= lists documents expiring within the horizon (already-expired ones included) for the organisation or one site.

## Fixed during the exercise

These were defects rather than product gaps, so they were corrected in the codebase as part of this run (the re-run reflects the fixes):

- **PH-02** – A worker login now ends when the worker record is deactivated (requireAuth and login check the linked worker). Cancelling or reassigning a leaver's future bookings remains open; see WF-02.
- **PT-04** – Worker logins can no longer read or edit colleagues' records; organisation-wide worker access needs a manager role.
- **PF-01** – Statement runs now bill every approved, un-invoiced timesheet up to the period end, so late approvals are caught up.
- **PR-01** – Site access notes are released only to an organisation with a confirmed booking on that shift.
- **PT-01** – New organisations register as PENDING and must be approved by the platform before they can publish or receive shifts.
- **PC-04** – Expiry alerts skip documents superseded by a newer verified one.
- **PM-05** – The release plan is kept when a shift starts or is cancelled, as the record of who was offered it and when.
- **PR-03** – When a cancellation reopens a filled shift, organisations whose stage is open are notified again.
- **PW-03** – Worker logins no longer see the inter-organisation charge rate as if it were their pay.
- **PH-01** – Workers have a home site (workers.homeSiteId, set from the import sheet or on the record); GET /workers?siteId= and the compliance reports filter by it.
- **PH-03** – A manager can be limited to some of the organisation's sites (decision 10): a site-limited manager sees and posts only those sites' shifts, sees only workers based there or unassigned, and cannot read or change pay rates or private notes.
- **PH-04** – GET /compliance/expiring?days= lists documents expiring within the horizon (already-expired ones included) for the organisation or one site.
- **PH-05** – Contracted hours count towards the 48-hour check by default; the employer records a worker's actual hours for any ISO week (PUT /workers/:id/weeks/:monday, in bulk via POST /workers/week-hours, or by naming the staff a surplus flag frees), and the agency reads hours and utilisation per bank worker from GET /finance/agency/bank-utilisation.
- **PR-02** – A host sees a "meets mandatory requirements" compliance status for each candidate and booked worker rather than the documents (decision 11), and can state its own requirements (GET/POST /compliance/requirements) against which suppliers declare their workers.
- **PR-05** – A pattern of shifts is posted as a series (POST /shifts/series, cancelled as one with POST /shifts/series/:id/cancel) and a single shift is copied with POST /shifts/:id/copy.
- **PA-01** – A platform-wide floor charge rate is derived from the minimum wage plus employer on-costs and the platform fee (GET /shifts/floor-rate, decision 3) and a shift cannot be posted below it; there is no rate card beyond the floor.
- **PA-02** – Timesheets left undecided for 14 days are approved automatically and picked up by the next settlement run, so a suspended or silent host's debt is invoiced and its suppliers are paid.
- **PA-04** – GET /finance/agency/pnl?from&to reports platform fees, own-bank sales, bank worker pay, gross margin and a per-client breakdown (decision 2); VAT and employer on-costs are not included.
- **PA-05** – GET /finance/agency/bank-utilisation?from&to reports hours worked and utilisation per bank worker over the range.
- **PF-02** – Host invoices and remittances are marked paid by the platform with a payment reference (POST /finance/host-invoices/:id/mark-paid, POST /finance/remittances/:id/mark-paid); the other party is notified and the audit trail records who did it.
- **PF-03** – A host queries a line on an issued invoice (POST /finance/credits naming the timesheet, amount and reason); the platform approves or rejects it and an approved credit nets off the next invoice and remittance (decision 5).
- **PF-06** – Submitted timesheets carry exception flags (late clock-in, over length, missing clock-out, submitted late), can be approved in bulk (POST /timesheets/bulk-approve), and auto-approve after 14 days of host silence.
- **PC-03** – Correcting a document supersedes the old version instead of editing it in place; the history stays on the record (and in the worker's export) and a verified document cannot be deleted.
- **PC-06** – Hosts define their own requirements, such as a site induction (POST /compliance/requirements), and record which workers meet them (POST /compliance/requirements/:id/workers).
- **PM-01** – A host bars a named worker from all or one of its sites with a reason (POST /trust/bars); future bookings with that host are released and the employer is told.
- **PM-02** – A host reports a concern about a lent worker to the employer (POST /trust/reports); the employer acknowledges and closes it, and the report stays on the worker's record (decision 14).
- **PS-01** – A pending request is a soft hold: a firm booking elsewhere takes priority and releases it, and the platform operator is reminded of requests unanswered for 12 hours or within 24 hours of the start (decision 7).
- **PT-03** – Authenticator-app two-factor is built in and compulsory for admins and managers, accounts lock after repeated failed sign-ins, passwords must be at least 10 characters and not common, and passwords can be changed or reset by email; Microsoft single sign-on is deferred to after go-live (decision 16).
- **PT-05** – GET /organisations/:id/audit?userId=&from=&to= filters the audit log by user and period.
