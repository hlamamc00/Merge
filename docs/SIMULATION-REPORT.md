# Merge six-month market-entry simulation

_Generated by `npm run sim -w @merge/api` (seed 42, 26 weeks from 2026-11-02, 2-hour steps). 68,178 API calls in 10.3 minutes of machine time. Re-run after changes to see what moved._

## What was simulated

A staffing agency ("Merge Staffing") launches the platform with a bank of 25 workers, growing to 45. Twenty care providers join over 18 weeks: two large groups (400 and 250 staff, 8 and 5 sites), six medium homes and twelve small services. Every worker is onboarded the way the platform allows today (one record, six to eight documents, a verification each). Each week every provider's staffing balance drifts with winter pressure (peaking mid-January) and random shocks; short-staffed providers post shifts with realistic lead times (55% three to ten days ahead, 25% one to three days, 20% same day), over-staffed providers raise surplus flags, suppliers and self-service workers check the board at their own rhythm and put eligible workers forward, hosts confirm first-come-first-served, workers clock in and out, hosts approve or dispute timesheets, and the agency issues monthly statements. Scripted events test a worker changing employer, a host blocking an unreliable supplier, the agency bank being released first for four weeks, a CQC-style records request, a non-paying host being suspended, a data-subject request, and a provider leaving.

Every action is a real HTTP call to the API as that person would make it, so "calls per task" below is a direct measure of how laborious the task is in the product today.

## What the numbers say

- **31% of shifts started with nobody confirmed.** 394 of 1269 shifts went unfilled. The network never had enough spare, compliant, in-range staff for winter demand, and nothing on the platform changed a shift's terms or reach when it was still open close to its start. 20% of shifts were posted within 12 hours of starting, which collapses the priority windows and turns the exchange into a scramble.
- **The agency bank filled 46% of bookings; priority access filled 19%.** Surplus flags did earn first access, but flagged organisations were small and their flags lapsed; the bank, which is always flagged in effect and checks the board more often, took the larger share. If the agency's aim is to grow a provider-to-provider exchange rather than its own agency business, the product needs to make flagging effortless and reward it.
- **Releasing the agency bank first did not improve fill rate; it displaced priority organisations and slowed filling.** Four weeks before the experiment: agency 39%, priority 25%, unfilled 32%, median time to fill 720 min. During (agency FIRST): agency 44%, priority 13%, unfilled 31%, time to fill 840 min. After: agency 42%, priority 16%, unfilled 34%, time to fill 720 min. The bank took bookings that flagged providers would otherwise have had, and hosts waited longer because the bank's exclusive hour delayed everyone else.
- **2 organisation(s) ended the run suspended or gone.** Lakeside Lodge, Birch House. Their open shifts, bookings and statements were left for people to sort out by hand; see PLT-01 and PLT-02.
- **32,804 notifications, none delivered outside the app.** Peak week 2027-03-08: 2,142 notifications across the network, mostly release notices. Every urgent shift, late cancellation and dispute relied on someone happening to log in.

## Gap register

3 blockers, 14 high, 8 medium, 0 low. Each gap records what the simulated person tried, what the platform did, and a proposed solution. Where the solution needs a product decision, it is listed under **Decisions needed** at the end.

### Communication

#### COM-01 · Notifications are in-app only, unbatched and unprioritised

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** everyone · **First hit:** month 7

- **What happened:** 32,804 notifications were generated over the run (SHIFT_AVAILABLE 18334, DOCUMENT_EXPIRING 4792, SHIFT_REQUEST 2928, SHIFT_DECLINED 1960). Northern Care Group received 74 on 2027-03-01. None left the app: no email, SMS or push exists, so an urgent shift released at 20:00 is only seen when someone next logs in.
- **Why it matters:** Urgent cover fails; important events (a late cancellation, a dispute) drown in release notices; people stop reading the bell.
- **Proposed solution:** Email/SMS/push channels with per-user preferences and quiet hours; digests for low-priority events (new shifts, statements) and immediate delivery for high-priority ones (urgent shift, cancellation, no-show, dispute); per-role routing so finance gets finance events.
- **Decision needed:** Which SMS provider and sender ID, and who pays for SMS (platform fee, or passed through to the organisation that opted in)?

### Onboarding

#### ONB-01 · No bulk import: every worker and every document is added one at a time

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** HR manager at a large provider · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 9×

- **What happened:** Northern Care Group with 400 staff needed 5715 API calls (one worker + 6–8 documents + a verification each). A human doing this through the UI would type each worker and each certificate by hand.
- **Why it matters:** Large providers will not finish onboarding; the platform only gets a handful of their staff, which starves the exchange of supply.
- **Proposed solution:** CSV/Excel import of workers (name, email, role, skills, contracted hours, postcode, document references and expiry dates) with a preview, validation report and partial-success handling; bulk verification; and an export of the same shape so providers can round-trip from their HR or rota system.

#### ONB-02 · Worker logins are created by hand with a password the manager must pass on

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** HR manager · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 21×

- **What happened:** Merge Staffing: the manager chose a password for each worker login and has no way to send it; there is no invitation email, no temporary-password flow and no password reset.
- **Why it matters:** Passwords travel by WhatsApp or paper, the same temporary password gets reused, and locked-out workers cannot help themselves.
- **Proposed solution:** Invite-by-email (also from the import): the worker sets their own password through a time-limited link; add forgotten-password reset and a forced change on first login. Needs an email channel.
- **Decision needed:** Which email provider (e.g. SendGrid, Postmark, SES) and sender domain should platform emails use?

#### ONB-04 · No password reset or email invitations

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** org admin / care worker · **First hit:** month 1

- **What happened:** POST /workers/import → 404; POST /auth/forgot-password → 404; POST /organisations/:id/invitations → 404. All three are routes a user would expect and none exist.
- **Why it matters:** Every lock-out becomes a support call to the agency; every new manager is created by hand with a shared password.
- **Proposed solution:** Invitation tokens and password-reset tokens delivered by email, with expiry and single use.

### Workforce

#### WF-03 · The 48-hour check ignores the worker's own rota: the platform only counts platform shifts

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** worker / employer (Working Time Regulations) · **First hit:** month 6

- **What happened:** 210 worker-weeks exceeded 48 hours once contracted hours are added to platform bookings, e.g. Zoe Robinson: 30h contracted + 44.0h on the platform; Megan Reid: 36h contracted + 40.5h on the platform; Kemi Lewis: 16h contracted + 40.0h on the platform; Amara Ward: 36h contracted + 40.0h on the platform; Jessica Robinson: 24h contracted + 33.0h on the platform. The eligibility rule has a field for internal hours but nothing feeds it.
- **Why it matters:** Fatigued workers, a regulatory breach for the employer, and an avoidable safeguarding risk at the host.
- **Proposed solution:** Count contracted hours by default (configurable per worker), let employers declare the worker's actual rota hours for the week (or import the rota), and show the running total before a request.
- **Decision needed:** Should the platform assume a worker is working their full contracted hours for their employer every week unless told otherwise?

#### ONB-03 · No site-level roles: a multi-site group gives every manager access to every site

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** Regional manager of a care group · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 2×

- **What happened:** Northern Care Group has 8 sites but one organisation; its rota manager login sees and can act on all of them.
- **Why it matters:** Groups cannot delegate to home managers safely; either they share one login or they register each home as a separate organisation and lose group-level reporting and internal-first dispatch.
- **Proposed solution:** Scope ORG_MANAGER to one or more sites; group-level admins see everything. Alternatively an organisation hierarchy (group → homes) with an internal-first dispatch stage.
- **Decision needed:** Should a care group be one organisation with site-scoped managers, or a parent organisation with child organisations that can still trade internally first?

#### WF-01 · Worker records are not portable between employers

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** HR manager · **First hit:** month 2

- **What happened:** Kamil Chen moved from Riverside Living to Oakwood Care Homes: the old record was deactivated and a new one typed in with 13 calls, re-entering 6 documents that had already been verified once. Ratings and history stayed with the old record.
- **Why it matters:** Duplicate identities, lost history, and repeated compliance work; the sector has high churn so this happens constantly.
- **Proposed solution:** A worker identity that is independent of employer (a "passport"), with an employment history; transfer with the worker's consent carries verified documents and ratings across.
- **Decision needed:** Should a worker's verified documents and ratings follow them to a new employer automatically (with their consent), or does each employer re-verify?

### Compliance

#### CMP-02 · Expiry alerts only reach the employer, and only in-app; workers cannot renew themselves

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** compliance officer · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 140×

- **What happened:** Northern Care Group had 34 active workers blocked by expired or missing documents at one point; renewals are entered by the officer after the fact (10 this round) because workers have no way to upload a renewed certificate.
- **Why it matters:** Supply is lost exactly when it is needed; the officer becomes the bottleneck for hundreds of certificates a year.
- **Proposed solution:** Worker self-upload with manager verification, email/SMS reminders to the worker at 60/30/7 days, and a compliance dashboard per organisation with upcoming expiries by month.

#### CMP-03 · Workers cannot upload their own documents or see their compliance status clearly

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** care worker (phone) · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 4×

- **What happened:** Harry Mensah tried to add a renewed training certificate from their own login: HTTP 403 (You do not have permission to do that). Documents are metadata only; there is no file upload at all.
- **Why it matters:** Every certificate goes through the manager, twice (receive it, type it in). Files live in email and shared drives, outside the audit trail.
- **Proposed solution:** Worker self-upload (photo/PDF) to object storage, OCR of the expiry date, manager verification queue, and a worker-facing compliance checklist.
- **Decision needed:** Where should document files be stored (e.g. S3-compatible bucket in the UK region) and how long should they be retained after a worker leaves?

#### CMP-01 · Verification backlog is invisible: unverified documents silently block workers

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** Compliance officer · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 21×

- **What happened:** Merge Staffing ended onboarding with 2 document(s) never verified; those workers show as non-compliant with no queue or reminder for the verifier.
- **Why it matters:** Supply is quietly lost and managers only find out when a request is refused.
- **Proposed solution:** A "needs verification" queue with bulk verify, plus a weekly digest of unverified and expiring documents per organisation.

### Finance

#### FIN-01 · Statements are generated and marked paid by hand; nothing collects money or chases it

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** agency finance · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 6×

- **What happened:** 6 statements issued this month; each host must be invoiced through the agency's own accounts system and marked paid manually; suppliers are paid outside the platform with no remittance.
- **Why it matters:** Double keying into the accounts package; no ageing report; the agency carries credit risk without visibility.
- **Proposed solution:** Self-billing PDFs and remittances, Xero/Sage/QuickBooks export, Direct Debit or card collection, automatic reminders at 7/14/30 days overdue, an ageing report, and a credit-hold rule that pauses posting for hosts over N days overdue.
- **Decision needed:** Who pays whom? (a) hosts pay the platform and the platform pays suppliers (platform carries the float), or (b) hosts pay suppliers directly and the platform only invoices its fee.

### Platform & admin

#### PLT-01 · Suspending an organisation does not withdraw its open shifts or settle its bookings

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** agency finance · **First hit:** month 5

- **What happened:** Lakeside Lodge suspended (200); its users are locked out (403) but 0 of its 0 open shifts stayed visible to Northern Care Group, and 0 confirmed booking(s) of its workers at other hosts stayed live with nobody able to manage them.
- **Why it matters:** Suppliers keep committing staff to a host that cannot pay; hosts keep relying on workers whose employer is locked out.
- **Proposed solution:** Suspension should pause posting, hide open shifts, notify counterparties about confirmed bookings and offer a grace window; a separate "credit hold" state for late payment that blocks new postings but keeps existing bookings.
- **Decision needed:** When a host is suspended for non-payment, should its already-confirmed future bookings be cancelled automatically, or honoured and invoiced in arrears?

#### TRS-01 · No reliability record: no-shows and late cancellations have no consequence beyond one host's memory

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** host admin · **First hit:** month 4

- **What happened:** Northern Care Group blocked Trafford Care Partnership after 2 no-shows. Other hosts cannot see that history; the platform charges nothing for it; 2 already-confirmed bookings with the blocked supplier stay in place with no warning. Over the run: 10 late cancellations and 20 no-shows network-wide.
- **Why it matters:** Unreliable suppliers keep winning priority access elsewhere; the agency cannot enforce its terms.
- **Proposed solution:** A reliability score per organisation and per worker (on-time clock-in, late cancellations, no-shows) visible to hosts and used as a dispatch weighting; configurable cancellation/no-show fees; and a prompt when blocking an organisation you still have bookings with.
- **Decision needed:** What should the late-cancellation and no-show penalties be (a fee, a lower priority rank, a suspension after N incidents), and who sets them: the platform or each host?

#### PLT-02 · No self-service offboarding; leaving means asking the agency to suspend you

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** org admin · **First hit:** month 6

- **What happened:** Birch House tried to leave: POST /organisations/:id/close → 404; setting its own status → 403. The agency had to suspend it, with 0 statement(s) still unpaid and no final reconciliation, data export or retention step.
- **Why it matters:** Messy exits, disputes over final statements, and data kept indefinitely.
- **Proposed solution:** A "leave the network" flow: stop new activity, run out existing bookings, final statement, data export, then archive after the retention period.

### Reporting

#### REP-01 · No regulator-ready record of who worked where with which checks

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** registered manager (CQC inspection) · **First hit:** month 4

- **What happened:** Asked for everyone who worked at Northern Care Group – Levenshulme between November and January with their DBS and training status: the report endpoint does not exist (404); assembling it by hand meant 27 calls, and each worker lookup was refused because the worker belongs to another organisation.
- **Why it matters:** Hosts cannot evidence safe staffing from the platform; this is a blocker for many registered managers adopting it.
- **Proposed solution:** A per-site workforce report (date range → worker, employer, role, shifts, document status on each shift date) as CSV/PDF, with the host allowed to see the compliance snapshot of workers who worked for it.

#### REP-02 · Reporting stops at a 30-day headline; no date ranges, no breakdown by site, role or counterparty, no CSV

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** finance clerk / agency owner · **First hit:** month 7

- **What happened:** Host stats return 10 fixed figures for the last 30 days only; format=csv is ignored (200, JSON). The agency overview (200) has network totals and fee for 30 days, with no per-client margin, no monthly series and no export.
- **Why it matters:** Finance teams rebuild the numbers in spreadsheets from statement PDFs; the agency cannot see which clients make money.
- **Proposed solution:** Report endpoints with date ranges and group-by (site, role, supplier/host, month), CSV export everywhere a table is shown, and a margin report for the agency.

### Security & data

#### SEC-01 · No data-subject tooling: export, erasure, consent to share, or retention rules

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** care worker / DPO · **First hit:** month 5

- **What happened:** Harry Mensah can read their profile (200) but there is no export (404) and no erasure (404). Nothing records that the worker consented to their profile, documents and ratings being shown to other organisations, and nothing deletes data after a worker leaves.
- **Why it matters:** The platform processes special-category data (DBS, health-related training) across organisations without the paperwork a data-protection officer will demand.
- **Proposed solution:** Consent capture at worker activation, a data export, an erasure/anonymisation job that keeps financial records, per-organisation retention settings, and a record of data-sharing agreements between organisations.
- **Decision needed:** Is the platform operator a data controller or a processor for worker data, and what retention period should apply after a worker or organisation leaves? (Needs a legal view.)

### Shifts & dispatch

#### DSP-01 · Nothing escalates an unfilled shift: no auto-widening, rate uplift or agency fallback

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** rota manager at a host · **First hit:** month 3

- **What happened:** 394 of 1269 shifts (31%) started with nobody confirmed. The only intervention available was an in-app warning 24 hours before; the rate, visibility and window never changed unless a manager edited the shift by hand.
- **Why it matters:** Hosts fall back to phoning agencies, which is exactly what the platform set out to replace.
- **Proposed solution:** Escalation playbooks per host: at T-48h/T-24h/T-6h widen visibility, raise the rate by a step, release to the agency bank, and send an SMS blast to eligible workers; show the host what will happen and when.
- **Decision needed:** Should rate uplifts on unfilled shifts be automatic (host sets a ceiling) or always a manual decision?

#### DSP-02 · No bulk confirm: each request is confirmed one by one

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** rota manager at a host · **First hit:** month 2 · **Recurred:** 6×

- **What happened:** Northern Care Group had 15 pending requests at one check; confirming them took 15 separate actions, and the first-come rule is enforced by the manager reading timestamps.
- **Why it matters:** Slow confirmations mean suppliers' workers sit in limbo and sometimes get booked elsewhere; winter weeks pile up hundreds of clicks.
- **Proposed solution:** Bulk confirm/decline with per-shift grouping, keyboard-fast review, and an optional auto-confirm policy per host (e.g. auto-confirm preferred partners or priority-tier requests).

#### DSP-03 · Flags must be re-raised by hand every fortnight; nothing nudges a supplier whose flag lapsed

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** rota manager at a supplier · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 41×

- **What happened:** Riverside Living still had spare staff but no active flag after its previous one expired or was fulfilled, so it had dropped to network timing without noticing.
- **Why it matters:** The priority mechanism depends on managers remembering to flag; in practice flags lapse and priority goes to whoever is most diligent, not most in need.
- **Proposed solution:** Expiry and fulfilment reminders, one-click re-raise, recurring flags (e.g. "every week until occupancy recovers"), and a suggestion to flag when the platform sees a supplier's workers repeatedly eligible but idle.

#### DSP-05 · The shift board is a flat list with no sort by fit, distance or pay

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** rota manager at a supplier · **First hit:** month 3 · **Recurred:** 54×

- **What happened:** Merge Staffing saw 28 open shifts at once; the API returns them ordered by release time only, and finding which ones its staff can actually cover means opening each one.
- **Why it matters:** Suppliers respond to the first few and miss better matches; priority organisations waste their window.
- **Proposed solution:** Board filters and sorting by role fit, distance, rate, start time; a per-organisation "you have N eligible workers" count computed server-side; saved searches and alerts.

#### DSP-06 · Shift times are instants, not local wall-clock times; the clock change silently changes shift lengths

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** rota manager · **First hit:** month 7

- **What happened:** 5 shift(s) straddled the switch to BST on 28 March 2027. A "19:00–07:00" night entered before the change is stored as instants, so after the change the UI shows it as 20:00–08:00 and pays 12 hours, while a real night across the change is 11 hours.
- **Why it matters:** Wrong paid hours twice a year, and confusion between what the rota says and what the platform shows.
- **Proposed solution:** Store shift times as local date-time plus the site time zone, compute instants on the fly, and show paid hours derived from the real elapsed time.

#### DSP-08 · Workers see shifts on their board that they cannot actually take

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** care worker (phone) · **First hit:** month 2

- **What happened:** Self-service workers had 142 requests refused after choosing a shift from their own board (top reasons: MISSING_SKILLS 77, NON_COMPLIANT 58, INSUFFICIENT_REST 8, OVERLAPPING_SHIFT 5). The worker board filters by role only; skills, compliance, rest and weekly hours are checked only when they press Request.
- **Why it matters:** The first thing a worker learns about the app is a rejection; they stop using it.
- **Proposed solution:** Evaluate eligibility server-side for the worker view and show only shifts they can take, with the blocked ones (and why) in a separate "not eligible" list.

### Scale & performance

#### SCL-01 · List endpoints return everything: no pagination, no date windows by default

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** agency ops / rota manager · **First hit:** month 7

- **What happened:** GET /shifts?scope=all returned 1269 rows in 48 ms; a large host's posted list with history returned 543 rows in 24 ms. Notifications, audit log, timesheets and assignments behave the same way.
- **Why it matters:** Pages get slower every month; on a real database over a network these become multi-second loads and large payloads on phones.
- **Proposed solution:** Cursor pagination with sensible default windows (e.g. this week), server-side filters, and summary endpoints for dashboards.

#### SCL-02 · Eligibility is recomputed for every worker on every look-up

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** rota manager at a large supplier · **First hit:** month 2

- **What happened:** "Find an eligible worker" ran 3,269 times with a p95 of 311 ms on an in-memory database; each call loads documents, bookings and unavailability for every worker of the organisation (up to 400) and evaluates them all, so the cost grows with headcount and with history.
- **Why it matters:** On a real database over the network the biggest employers, who matter most for supply, get the slowest screens.
- **Proposed solution:** Pre-filter in SQL by role cover, shareable and active; cache each worker's compliance snapshot and weekly hours; evaluate the remaining candidates only.
