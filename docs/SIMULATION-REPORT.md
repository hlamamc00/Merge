# Merge six-month market-entry simulation

_Generated by `npm run sim -w @merge/api` (seed 42, 26 weeks from 2026-11-02, 2-hour steps). 67,824 API calls in 9.2 minutes of machine time. Re-run after changes to see what moved._

## What was simulated

A staffing agency ("Merge Staffing") launches the platform with a bank of 25 workers, growing to 45. Twenty care providers join over 18 weeks: two large groups (400 and 250 staff, 8 and 5 sites), six medium homes and twelve small services. Every worker is onboarded the way the platform allows today (one record, six to eight documents, a verification each). Each week every provider's staffing balance drifts with winter pressure (peaking mid-January) and random shocks; short-staffed providers post shifts with realistic lead times (55% three to ten days ahead, 25% one to three days, 20% same day), over-staffed providers raise surplus flags, suppliers and self-service workers check the board at their own rhythm and put eligible workers forward, hosts confirm first-come-first-served, workers clock in and out, hosts approve or dispute timesheets, and the agency issues monthly statements. Scripted events test a worker changing employer, a host blocking an unreliable supplier, the agency bank being released first for four weeks, a CQC-style records request, a non-paying host being suspended, a data-subject request, and a provider leaving.

Every action is a real HTTP call to the API as that person would make it, so "calls per task" below is a direct measure of how laborious the task is in the product today.

## What the numbers say

- **31% of shifts started with nobody confirmed.** 406 of 1299 shifts went unfilled. The network never had enough spare, compliant, in-range staff for winter demand, and nothing on the platform changed a shift's terms or reach when it was still open close to its start. 20% of shifts were posted within 12 hours of starting, which collapses the priority windows and turns the exchange into a scramble.
- **The agency bank filled 45% of bookings; priority access filled 19%.** Surplus flags did earn first access, but flagged organisations were small and their flags lapsed; the bank, which is always flagged in effect and checks the board more often, took the larger share. If the agency's aim is to grow a provider-to-provider exchange rather than its own agency business, the product needs to make flagging effortless and reward it.
- **Releasing the agency bank first did not improve fill rate; it displaced priority organisations and slowed filling.** Four weeks before the experiment: agency 34%, priority 17%, unfilled 29%, median time to fill 720 min. During (agency FIRST): agency 41%, priority 17%, unfilled 32%, time to fill 840 min. After: agency 47%, priority 14%, unfilled 42%, time to fill 720 min. The bank took bookings that flagged providers would otherwise have had, and hosts waited longer because the bank's exclusive hour delayed everyone else.
- **2 organisation(s) ended the run suspended or gone.** Oakwood Care Homes, Birch House. Their open shifts, bookings and statements were left for people to sort out by hand; see PLT-01 and PLT-02.
- **30,422 notifications, none delivered outside the app.** Peak week 2027-03-08: 2,263 notifications across the network, mostly release notices. Every urgent shift, late cancellation and dispute relied on someone happening to log in.

### Communication

#### COM-01 · Notifications are in-app only, unbatched and unprioritised

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** everyone · **First hit:** month 7

- **What happened:** 30,422 notifications were generated over the run (SHIFT_AVAILABLE 18927, SHIFT_REQUEST 2861, SHIFT_DECLINED 1920, DOCUMENT_EXPIRING 1803). Northern Care Group received 85 on 2027-02-01. None left the app: no email, SMS or push exists, so an urgent shift released at 20:00 is only seen when someone next logs in. Persona walkthrough (Care worker on a phone (self-service login at Riverside Living)): Live checks as Harry (harry.mensah.730@riverside-living.example):
- GET /api/v1/notifications?limit=200 returned 200 with unread 19 and 19 rows. All 19 are SHIFT_CONFIRMED (the finding saw 16 and then 18; other runs have added rows). Every title is "Confirmed/Booked: Harry Mensah at <host>".
- **Why it matters:** Urgent cover fails; important events (a late cancellation, a dispute) drown in release notices; people stop reading the bell.
- **Proposed solution:** Email/SMS/push channels with per-user preferences and quiet hours; digests for low-priority events (new shifts, statements) and immediate delivery for high-priority ones (urgent shift, cancellation, no-show, dispute); per-role routing so finance gets finance events.
- **Decision needed:** Which SMS provider and sender ID, and who pays for SMS (platform fee, or passed through to the organisation that opted in)?

#### PC-04 · Expiry alerts fire for documents that have already been renewed: 21 of the 37 due this month are superseded

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** Compliance officer at Trafford Care Partnership (250 staff, surplus supplier) · **First hit:** month 6 · **Status:** ✅ fixed during the exercise

- **What happened:** Trafford, 250 ACTIVE workers; clock 2027-05-03T08:00Z. I pulled GET /workers/:id for all 250 and compared with GET /notifications and a read-only query of apps/api/data/sim.db.

1. The scheduler's selection (expiresAt <= now+30d and > now-1d) matches 38 worker_documents rows. 22 of them have another verified document of the same type with a later expiry (e.g. Rahim Ali MANDATORY_TRAINING 2027-05-14, superseded by one valid to 2029-08-17, renewal created and verified 2027-04-28; Sam Begum PHOTO_ID; Priya Byrne RIGHT_TO_WORK; Carys Chen RIGHT_TO_WORK; Tariq Davies MEDICATION_TRAINING). 16 are genuine and equal the 16 workers with an EXPIRING item in complianceSummary. The 38 rows are for 34 workers, and the 22 superseded rows are for 20 workers. None are unverified.

2. …
- **Why it matters:** More than half of the compliance alerts are wrong, every week, for the person whose job is to act on them; after a month of chasing renewed certificates an officer stops reading them, which defeats the alerting that CMP-02 and COM-01 rely on.
- **Proposed solution:** Smallest fix, about one function. In alertExpiringDocuments, group the worker's documents by type and alert only when the best document for that type (the same ranking as pickBestDocument, exported from packages/shared) is itself in the EXPIRING status and its expiresAt is within the horizon. Skip any type the worker's role does not require, as well as already-renewed rows. Dedupe on (workerId, type) instead of documentId so a renewal chain does not re-alert. Optionally, when a verified renewal is saved (POST/PATCH /workers/:id/documents), mark that worker's open DOCUMENT_EXPIRING notifications for the type as read.

Follow-ups, each separable: (a) add a type filter and an offset or before-cursor to GET /notifications, so expiry alerts can be listed on their own; (b) replace the per-document alerts with one weekly digest per organisation listing worker, type and days left, each with a link to the worker page (this is the COM-01 angle); (c) add a test that seeds a superseded document and asserts no alert, since the existing tests cover only the eligibility warning.
- **Decision needed:** None required for the bug fix. For the digest, decide whether the alert should be keyed on the role-required document set only, or on any document a worker holds. Tie this to the CMP-02 decision on who is notified.

#### PR-04 · No way to contact the booked worker or their employer, and no message channel on a booking

**Severity:** 🟨 Medium · **Seen by:** Rota manager at Meadow View (18 staff, one small home) · **First hit:** month 6

- **What happened:** - Live API as admin@meadow-view.example:
  - GET /assignments/asg_z3fd5ip3ibq57edl returns 200 with worker = {id, firstName, lastName, jobRole, skills}. There is no phone or email. The assignment is now status CANCELLED, cancelledByOrgId org_oyto16asbisuoy2f, reason "Worker needed on our own rota".
  - GET /organisations/org_oyto16asbisuoy2f returns 200 with {id, name, type, serviceTypes, city, postcode, status}: no phone, email or address. …
- **Why it matters:** At 18:00 with a sick night carer the rota manager can post a shift and then only wait; they cannot phone the organisation that holds the priority flag, chase a request, or tell tomorrow's worker which door to use without exposing the instructions to everyone (see the access-notes finding). This is distinct from COM-01 (notification channels): even with email and SMS, there is no counterpart to contact.
- **Proposed solution:** Smallest useful step, in two stages.

1. Contact exposure on a live relationship.
- Make organisations.phone a required "duty phone" at registration, or prompt for it in Settings.
- When a host and supplier share a REQUESTED, OFFERED or CONFIRMED assignment, return {dutyPhone, email} from GET /organisations/:id and add a `contact` block to the assignment and shift-detail responses. Do this in both directions, and only while the booking is live.
- Share the worker's own phone only if workers.shareable is true or the employer sets a per-organisation "share worker phone on confirmation" flag.
- Show a "Contact" card on ShiftDetailPage.

2. Per-booking thread.
- Add one table, assignment_messages (assignmentId, fromUserId, fromOrgId, body, createdAt).
- Add GET and POST /assignments/:id/messages, open to the host manager, the supplier manager and the booked worker.
- Raise a notification on each message, so it reuses the COM-01 channels once those exist.
- Add a "Brief worker" action on a confirmed booking: a private note stored on the assignment and visible only to the supplier and the worker. This also lets site.accessNotes be withheld until confirmation (the separate access-notes finding).
- **Decision needed:** Under the inter-organisation data-sharing terms, may a worker's personal phone number be shown to the host at all, or only the supplier's duty number? If workers' numbers may be shared, is consent per worker (the shareable flag), per employer, or per booking? Is a duty phone mandatory for supplier organisations? Should message threads be retained for the audit trail (with a retention period), or deleted after the shift?

### Onboarding

#### ONB-01 · No bulk import: every worker and every document is added one at a time

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** HR manager at a large provider · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 9×

- **What happened:** Northern Care Group with 400 staff needed 5716 API calls (one worker + 6–8 documents + a verification each). A human doing this through the UI would type each worker and each certificate by hand.
- **Why it matters:** Large providers will not finish onboarding; the platform only gets a handful of their staff, which starves the exchange of supply.
- **Proposed solution:** CSV/Excel import of workers (name, email, role, skills, contracted hours, postcode, document references and expiry dates) with a preview, validation report and partial-success handling; bulk verification; and an export of the same shape so providers can round-trip from their HR or rota system.

#### ONB-02 · Worker logins are created by hand with a password the manager must pass on

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** HR manager · **First hit:** month 1 · **Recurred:** 21×

- **What happened:** Merge Staffing: the manager chose a password for each worker login and has no way to send it; there is no invitation email, no temporary-password flow and no password reset.
- **Why it matters:** Passwords travel by WhatsApp or paper, the same temporary password gets reused, and locked-out workers cannot help themselves.
- **Proposed solution:** Invite-by-email (also from the import): the worker sets their own password through a time-limited link; add forgotten-password reset and a forced change on first login. Needs an email channel.
- **Decision needed:** Which email provider (e.g. SendGrid, Postmark, SES) and sender domain should platform emails use?

#### ONB-04 · No password reset or email invitations

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** org admin / care worker · **First hit:** month 1

- **What happened:** POST /workers/import → 404; POST /auth/forgot-password → 404; POST /organisations/:id/invitations → 404. All three are routes a user would expect and none exist. Persona walkthrough (Care worker on a phone (self-service login at Riverside Living)): I re-ran everything live as Harry Mensah (usr_tge3l2c7ekendxf5, org_oyto16asbisuoy2f). **Self-service routes.**
- POST /auth/forgot-password, /auth/password-reset, /auth/reset-password, /auth/change-password and /auth/password all return 404. - PATCH /auth/me returns 404.
- **Why it matters:** Every lock-out becomes a support call to the agency; every new manager is created by hand with a shared password.
- **Proposed solution:** Invitation tokens and password-reset tokens delivered by email, with expiry and single use.

### Security & data

#### PT-01 · Open sign-up gives an unchecked stranger care-home addresses and staff door codes in three calls

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** IT and data-protection lead evaluating Merge for a provider group · **First hit:** month 6 · **Status:** ✅ fixed during the exercise

- **What happened:** Checked live at http://localhost:4055 and in the code.

1. Unauthenticated GET /organisations, /workers, /shifts, /capacity/network and /auth/me all return 401. That is correct behaviour.

2. POST /auth/register needs no auth and returned 201 with organisation.status ACTIVE plus an ORG_ADMIN token.
   - The code is at apps/api/src/routes/auth.ts, lines 56-69. It hard-codes status:'ACTIVE' at line 66.
   - The password "password" was accepted. zAdmin only requires min(8) characters, with no strength check.
   - cqcRegistrationNumber is nullable and optional (auth.ts line 17). My probe registered without one.
   - No rate limit exists. A grep for rateLimit, express-rate and helmet in apps/api/src finds nothing.

3. Before any flag, the new organisation's GET /shifts?scope=available returned 0 rows. …
- **Why it matters:** Anyone on the internet can, within seconds, learn where vulnerable residents live, how to get in through the staff door and whom to ask for. A data-protection lead cannot accept that. It also turns the shift board into a reconnaissance tool and lets an attacker confirm which staff emails to target for phishing.
- **Proposed solution:** Smallest sensible change, in priority order.

1. Register creates the organisation as PENDING. A platform admin approves it by setting status ACTIVE through the existing PATCH /organisations/:id. The engine already skips non-ACTIVE organisations. Also make lib/auth.ts block a PENDING organisation from posting shifts or raising flags until it is approved. Show a "waiting for approval" screen. Make the CQC number mandatory at registration, and add a minimum password strength rule. Add a rate limit on /auth/register and /auth/login.

2. Split the site payload. siteForGuest should return only name, serviceType, city and postcode area, with lat/lng coarsened or rounded to about 1 km.
   - Return the full addressLine1, exact lat/lng and accessNotes only when the viewer is the host, a platform admin, or the supplier or worker holds a CONFIRMED assignment on that shift.
   - Apply this to scope=available, scope=supplied and GET /shifts/:id.
   - Optionally release the full address once the viewer's tier is open and a request is made, but never before.
   - Update the ShiftDetailPage block that shows access notes so it shows them only when an assignment is confirmed.

3. Make register respond the same whether or not the email exists, for example 202 "check your email", or collapse into the approval flow from item 1.

4. Gate the demo-login box with import.meta.env.DEV, and remove the seeded demo accounts from production seeding.

5. Add a hint on the Sites access-notes field: "Do not store door codes here; give them to confirmed workers directly."
- **Decision needed:** What checks must a new organisation pass before it joins the network and sees any shifts or site details? The options are a CQC registration check, a signed data-sharing or data-processing agreement, and manual approval by the operator. Who carries out those checks and who owns the approval queue? Separately, should the precise address be shown before a worker is confirmed, and what is the policy for door codes: are they shared only inside the platform after confirmation, or kept out of the platform entirely?

#### PT-03 · No Microsoft sign-in, no two-factor, no lockout, weak passwords allowed, passwords that can never be changed, and leavers can still log in

**Severity:** 🟥 Blocker · **Seen by:** IT and data-protection lead evaluating Merge for a provider group · **First hit:** month 6

- **What happened:** Checked live against http://localhost:4055/api/v1 and in the code.

SSO and MFA:
- GET /auth/sso, /auth/oauth/microsoft, /auth/saml/metadata and /auth/oidc/callback all return 404.
- POST /auth/mfa/enroll and /auth/2fa/setup return 404.
- PATCH /organisations/org_1a4shuzibdsu6f4n with {requireMfa:true, ssoProvider:"microsoft", allowedEmailDomain:"northern-care-group.example"} returned 200 and the org object came back without those fields. …
- **Why it matters:** For a provider group that has to complete the Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT), a cloud system holding DBS data that admins reach with a password alone (no 2FA), with no single sign-on and no brute-force protection, will not pass. Staff need yet another password, and when someone leaves the group's Microsoft account switch-off does not reach Merge. Leavers keep access unless someone remembers a second screen.
- **Proposed solution:** Smallest sensible slice, in order of effort:
1. Deactivating a worker also sets the linked users.status to DISABLED, and re-activating restores it. This is a few lines in PATCH /workers/:id and could ship with WF-02.
2. Add POST /auth/change-password (current and new password) and an admin-reset endpoint on PATCH /organisations/:id/users/:userId. Fix the 500 on an empty update body. Add users.tokenVersion and put it in the JWT so password changes and "sign out all sessions" invalidate existing tokens. Add POST /auth/logout that bumps the version.
3. Password policy: minimum 12 characters, a breach-list or common-password check, and a shared zPassword validator used in register, worker login, org user create and change-password.
4. Login throttling: per-email and per-IP attempt counter, a temporary lockout with a Retry-After header, and a LOGIN_FAILED audit event.
5. Organisation setting requireMfa, with TOTP enrolment and challenge at login, enforced for ORG_ADMIN, ORG_MANAGER and PLATFORM_ADMIN.
6. OIDC sign-in with Microsoft Entra per organisation, tied to allowedEmailDomain, with an optional "SSO only" switch that disables password login for that org.
Items 1 to 4 are small and local. 5 and 6 are the larger pieces and need the decision below.
- **Decision needed:** Should 2FA be compulsory for every admin and manager on the platform, or an option each organisation turns on? Which identity providers are needed at launch (Microsoft Entra only, or Google too)? Should SSO-enforced organisations lose password login entirely? What lockout policy should apply, for example attempts, window and unlock method, given that password reset by email does not exist yet (ONB-04)?

#### PA-03 · Platform admin is all-or-nothing: a junior ops user sees only the agency bank, and any second admin can lock out the owner unnoticed

**Severity:** 🟧 High · **Seen by:** Owner of Merge Staffing (the agency that runs the platform and its own bank) · **First hit:** month 6

- **What happened:** Throwaway accounts were created in the agency organisation org_w9x3e2yunieke54o and in Itdp Probe Care, then disabled or demoted; the real owner was not touched.

Code:
- apps/api/src/routes/admin.ts:15 gates the whole router with requireRole(PLATFORM_ADMIN).
- apps/api/src/lib/auth.ts lines 62-72 define the role sets and make assertOrgAccess pass for any platform admin.
- apps/api/src/routes/organisations.ts around lines 230-262: POST /:id/users accepts role PLATFORM_ADMIN from any platform admin into any organisation. …
- **Why it matters:** Every ops person who must help a client has to be given control of fees, suspensions, statements and other admins; the owner has no protection against a second admin and would not learn of a lock-out, fee change or suspension until a client phones.
- **Proposed solution:** Smallest sensible fix, in three steps:

1. Protect the owner and close the escalation. Add settings.ownerUserId and have the users PATCH route refuse to disable or demote the owner except by the owner. Block non-platform-admin callers (ORG_ADMIN of the agency) from PATCHing PLATFORM_ADMIN users. Allow PLATFORM_ADMIN creation only in the agency organisation (settings.ownerOrgId). Accept PLATFORM_ADMIN in the PATCH role enum for platform-admin callers, and show Disable and Role controls for platform-admin rows in the UI, hidden for the owner row.
2. Raise a notification to every other active platform admin, and email once COM-01 lands, on platform-admin create, disable and demote, fee or setting change, and organisation suspension. Include the actor and the new value.
3. Introduce platformRole values (OWNER, OPS_READ, OPS_ACT, FINANCE) mapped to permission sets, replacing the blanket requireRole(PLATFORM_ADMIN) on admin routes. OPS_READ would cover read-only cross-organisation views, OPS_ACT would allow acting for an organisation, and FINANCE would cover invoices and fees. Add MFA or re-authentication for settings, suspension and admin-management endpoints.

Steps 1 and 2 are small, and step 1 closes the lock-out and escalation holes on its own. Step 3 is a bigger piece of work.
- **Decision needed:** Which permission sets does the agency need on day one (for example owner, ops act-for-organisation, ops read-only, finance), and should sensitive actions such as fee changes, organisation suspension and disabling another admin require a second admin's approval? The outcome categories (for example "ran short with a risk assessment") should be agreed with the registered manager and compliance lead.
23. **PA-03** – Which permission sets does the agency need on day one (for example owner, ops act-for-organisation, ops read-only, finance), and should sensitive actions such as fee changes, organisation suspension and disabling another admin require a second admin's approval? Should the owner be a single named user or any admin in a designated owner group? Should platform admins be restricted to the agency organisation only?
24. **PH-02** – Whether a leaver's confirmed future bookings are auto-cancelled (host notified, shift reopened to the network, with the cancellation exempt from late-cancellation penalties) or flagged for the rota manager to re-cover first. Also whether an ORG_MANAGER (rota manager) may disable a worker's login or only an org admin, and whether setting a worker INACTIVE should always disable the login (with re-enabling on reactivation) or whether some employers want leavers to keep read-only access to payslips and history.
25. **PR-01** – Should a supplier manager see arrival instructions before confirmation, to brief a worker they are about to put forward, or only once the assignment is CONFIRMED? The code comments, seed data and Settings copy point to confirmation only. Related choices: whether notes stay visible after cancellation or completion, and whether the field should be split into public directions and confidential codes.
26. **PT-02** – Should the operator (the agency that runs Merge, which is also a supplier on the network) be able to see tenants' worker pay rates, notes and ID or DBS references at all? If yes, on what lawful basis and DPA terms, and is it acting as processor or controller (this links to the SEC-01 question)? Does the tenant, rather than the operator, own and hold the audit trail of access to its data? And should the agency's commercial arm be run from a separate organisation or login from the platform-operator role?
27. **PT-04** – Should a worker see their own hourlyPayRate? Should they see the employer's private notes about them? Showing notes is a UK GDPR subject-access question and may need HR review before the field is exposed, so the safe default is to strip notes from the worker's own view and handle access to them through the data-subject work in SEC-01.
28. **PT-05** – 1) Should tenants see only actions taken by their own users, or also actions agency staff took on their data (for example the agency verifying a document for them)? 2) How long should audit rows be kept, and does logging reads of worker records (high volume, special-category data) justify its storage cost? This ties to the controller-versus-processor and retention question already open under SEC-01. 3) Should org managers have access, or org admins only?
29. **SEC-01** – Is the platform operator a data controller or a processor for worker data, and what retention period should apply after a worker or organisation leaves? (Needs a legal view.)
30. **DSP-01** – Should rate uplifts on unfilled shifts be automatic (host sets a ceiling) or always a manual decision?
31. **PR-05** – Product decisions needed. (1) Should a repeat be materialised as N independent shifts, which is simplest and keeps today's edit and cancel semantics, or as a series object with 'edit this / edit all' and 'cancel the series'? (2) Should a batch publish all shifts at once, or stage drafts and publish week by week? Publishing a month ahead starts dispatch and release for every shift immediately. (3) What is the cap on shifts per batch and on how far ahead the repeat can run? (4) Should templates live per site or per organisation?
32. **PS-01** – How long may an unanswered request hold a worker before it lapses: a fixed number of hours after the request, or a point before the shift start (for example 12 h after, or 24 h before)? Should the hold be soft (a firm booking elsewhere wins, first confirm wins) or should the platform keep blocking and just auto-release? And what, if anything, should happen to a host that persistently ignores requests: only show the stat, rank its shifts lower on the board, prompt it to switch on instant book, or eventually pause its shifts?
33. **ONB-03** – Should a care group be one organisation with site-scoped managers, or a parent organisation with child organisations that can still trade internally first?
34. **PH-01** – 1) Does a worker belong to exactly one home, or can float/bank staff attach to several? A single nullable homeSiteId is the smallest option; a join table is the flexible one. 2) Should a home move be dated (effective-from history) so past reports stay attributed to the old home, or is a plain overwrite acceptable at first? 3) Should the home be visible to host organisations (currently workers are shown to hosts without private fields), or private to the employer like hourlyPayRate?
35. **WF-01** – Should a worker's verified documents and ratings follow them to a new employer automatically (with their consent), or does each employer re-verify?
36. **PR-04** – Under the inter-organisation data-sharing terms, may a worker's personal phone number be shown to the host at all, or only the supplier's duty number? If workers' numbers may be shared, is consent per worker (the shareable flag), per employer, or per booking? Is a duty phone mandatory for supplier organisations? Should message threads be retained for the audit trail (with a retention period), or deleted after the shift?
37. **PC-06** – Does a site induction belong to the host or the site (the host sets and sees it, and the supplier gets read-only visibility), or does it travel with the worker as part of their record (links to WF-01)? Decisions that follow from the first one: whether it is per organisation or per site for multi-site groups (links to ONB-03), whether it expires, and whether a missing induction blocks a booking or only warns.
38. **PH-04** – Should the expiring threshold be one platform-wide default (for example 60 days) or configurable per organisation and per document type? DBS, right to work and training have different renewal lead times. Should the report cover only the organisation's own workers, or also lent-in workers whose documents the host cannot currently see?
39. **PA-02** – (1) Should SUBMITTED timesheets auto-approve after N days of host silence (the same decision as FIN-02), and what is N? Should a suspension also approve or settle that host's outstanding SUBMITTED items, or leave them for the PA to approve on the host's behalf? (2) Who carries credit risk when a host does not pay: the agency, which guarantees suppliers and absorbs bad debt, or the supplier, with the platform only invoicing its fee? This is the same question as FIN-01's 'who pays whom' and decides whether auto-approval is safe to enable. (3) Should a credit-hold host keep its confirmed future bookings honoured (invoiced in arrears), as PLT-01 asks, or have them cancelled?
40. **PA-04** – Three product and accounting decisions are needed. (1) Is the agency's own bank supply a sale by the agency, with gross revenue and worker pay as cost of sales, or does it stay modelled as a supplier statement like any other? That means deciding whether the 8% platform fee should be charged on bank shifts at all, since today the agency pays itself. (2) Is chargeRate net or gross of VAT, and what is the VAT treatment of the staff supply (supplier or agency as principal) versus the platform fee? Care-home hosts often cannot reclaim VAT, so this changes what they pay. (3) Which date decides the reporting month: shift start, approval or statement period? These give different April figures today (£9,771 vs £9,933). Also confirm whether the pay-rate snapshot should include employer on-costs (NI, pension, holiday pay), since raw hourlyPayRate understates the true cost of sales.
41. **PF-02** – Who is allowed to record payment: (1) only the payer (host), with the payee confirming receipt or disputing it, both notified; (2) either party, as today, but with the marker recorded, the counterparty notified, and the flag reversible; or (3) capture automatically from a collection provider (roadmap item 4 and FIN-01), after which the manual flag becomes an exception route. This depends on the FIN-01 decision on who pays whom. If hosts pay the platform and the platform pays suppliers, only the platform should set PAID. If hosts pay suppliers directly, the host records payment and the supplier confirms it.
42. **PF-05** – 1) Who signs off: any org admin, or a named approver per site? ONB-03, no site-level roles, constrains the second option. 2) What the cap counts: committed bookings (shifts times rate times headcount, so it blocks before commitment) or approved timesheet spend (simpler, but only warns after the money is committed). 3) Which date defines "month" for a budget: shift start or timesheet approval. The two give different totals today. 4) Whether a breach hard-blocks publishing or soft-warns with an override reason.
43. **PF-06** – (a) Is auto-approval after a silence period acceptable to hosts, and what is N and the default (off, 14 days)? (b) How long after a shift ends may a supplier still submit times, and can the host override? (c) Should a neutral "close without pay" outcome exist for a booking nobody worked, or does the host have to use no-show (which counts against the worker and supplier)? (d) May the new finance role approve or dispute hours, or only read? The finding argues that someone who cannot verify the hours should not approve them.
44. **PA-01** – Is the floor a platform-wide rule, a per-host negotiated rate card, or both? Does it hard-reject below-floor shifts or only warn? Does the night uplift raise the host's charge, the supplier's or worker's pay, or both? Should the floor apply to other suppliers' posting hosts, or only to shifts the agency's own bank can fill?
45. **PA-06** – Should rating be a required step of timesheet approval (on both the host and supplier side) or an optional prompt? Who may see supplier-to-host ratings: the platform admin only, the rated host, or other suppliers?
46. **PM-06** – Should Merge hold the internal rota itself (import or integrate with rota systems), or only record headline own-staff numbers per shift or daypart so ratios can be calculated? The recommended starting point is headline numbers only, because it avoids making Merge a rota system and the personal data that brings.
47. **PH-03** – Define the home-manager role. Option 1: rota-only, with no pay, notes or site editing, which is the safest default for delegating to home managers. Option 2: full HR for their own site only, which needs the site scope from ONB-03 plus a worker-to-site link. A related question: should pay visibility be a separate permission or flag rather than tied to the ORG_ADMIN role?
48. **PH-06** – When a home, group or worker is paused, what should happen to lending that is already in flight?
(a) Do nothing to existing bookings: only new requests are blocked.
(b) Also auto-withdraw REQUESTED and OFFERED (not yet CONFIRMED) assignments and notify the host.
(c) Also withdraw the organisation's or site's ACTIVE SURPLUS flags.
CONFIRMED bookings should stay and be cancelled deliberately, because cancelling them triggers late-cancellation records.
Note that today confirmAssignment does not re-check `shareable`, so (a) is not even a safe default: a host can confirm a REQUESTED assignment after the pause. At minimum confirmation should re-check the pause. Related: WF-02 for deactivated workers.
A second product question is whether the pause should be reason-coded (for example "short-staffed", "outbreak" or "CQC visit") for audit, and whether a pause should require org admin rather than manager rights.
49. **PR-03** – Whether a supplier cancellation should automatically re-announce to every organisation with access, or only to the priority and agency tiers first (re-running the staged priority window), or only when the shift is close enough to the start (for example inside 72h) to avoid notification noise. This interacts with COM-01, because the in-app inbox is already unbatched and unprioritised.
50. **PR-06** – Should a host's declared shortage alter dispatch for its shifts (for example notify matching surplus organisations first, or lift the host's preferred partners), or only drive recommendations and demand visibility? Also: may the ORG_MANAGER role add preferred partners, or does that stay admin-only?
51. **PW-05** – 1. Are clock times attendance evidence or only a convenience for entering timesheets? If evidence, the window, future-date rejection and geofence are required and /submit must be constrained too. If convenience, a variance flag at approval is enough.
2. Who owns the on-the-day contact: a per-site field the host fills in, the host organisation's phone, or the agency or supplier manager? And should arrival details (keypad codes, names, phone) be released only once a booking is confirmed, or closer to the start time?
3. What should the default clock-in window be, and what should happen outside it: block, warn, or flag for the host?
52. **PA-05** – Is the utilisation denominator contracted hours or max hours per week? Contracted hours is 0 or empty for 24 of 45 bank workers, so utilisation is undefined for over half the bank unless contracted hours becomes required or zero-hours workers are treated as a separate group with an hours-offered measure. Should 'hours worked' count approved timesheet hours (accurate, but lags approval) or booked shift hours (immediate, but includes no-shows and late cancellations)?
53. **PH-05** – Should contracted hours only drive surplus suggestions (advisory, with the manager still typing the flag), or also cap lending, meaning no lending beyond contracted plus an allowance? And should the platform assume a worker works their full contracted hours for their employer each week unless told otherwise (the WF-03 decision), since the hours-short figure depends on that?
54. **PS-06** – Which site facilities to standardise. Whether worker conditions are hard blockers or warnings that a manager can override. Whether a worker can edit their own conditions or only their employer can. Also whether the distance warning, which already fires for 195 of 250 workers on the sampled shift, should be tightened first so a new warning does not add to the noise.
55. **PW-04** – 1) Which profile fields the worker owns versus the employer: phone and email (probably the worker), postcode and travel radius (worker proposes, or manager approves, since they drive distance matching and may be contractual), and skills, shareable, pay rate and hours (stay employer-only). 2) Whether a new unavailability or recurring rule that collides with REQUESTED/OFFERED bookings should auto-withdraw those pending requests, or only warn the worker and notify the manager. 3) Whether CONFIRMED bookings are ever auto-cancelled; recommend never, and warn only, so that cancellations stay inside the late-cancellation process in TRS-01. 4) Whether the cross-worker access defect in point 5 of the evidence is raised as its own security gap. It looks worth a separate entry because it is a privacy issue (colleague pay rates) and not just a usability one.
